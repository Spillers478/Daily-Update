In Ukraine Civilians Die at Alarming Rates Amid Intensifying Attacks, UN Human Rights Officials Say Kyiv, November 17, 2026 -- Powerful weapons of war kill ever growing numbers of civilians across Ukraine, the UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine (HRMMU) said today. Attacks in October alone left at least 372 civilians dead and 1,981 injured HRMMU reported in its monthly update. The report notes that the number of killed and injured in October of this year was 95 per cent higher than in October 2025. The latest figures show that this year has seen significantly higher casualties than previous years. The alarming trend continued in November which saw one of the deadliest single attacks for civilians since the start of Russia's full-scale invasion. On 2 November multiple bombs struck the premises of a school, including an underground shelter in Komyshuvakha in south-eastern Ukraine, where civilians had gathered to receive humanitarian assistance. Ukrainian authorities announced on 16 November that 72 people had died in the attack and that four others were still missing. HRMMU continues to verify the identity and status of those killed, but has already confirmed that dozens of them were civilians, including local residents, local administration staff and emergency workers. "The first ten months of this year have seen more civilian deaths than all of 2025, which in turn was worse than the year before," said Danielle Bell who heads HRMMU. "The world should not become numb to the deadly toll the war exacts on Ukraine's civilians," she said. Long-range weapons (missiles and drones) remain the leading cause of civilian casualties, accounting for more than half of those killed and injured. In October, casualties from aerial glide bombs increased, with the number of civilians killed doubling compared with September, reaching 92 killed and 331 injured. Aerial glide bombs and short-range drones accounted for nearly equal shares of civilian casualties, 18 and 19 per cent, respectively. In October, Russian armed forces significantly intensified attacks on energy infrastructure across Ukraine as the country braces for its fifth winter at war. The attacks damaged electricity and heating infrastructure, temporarily disrupting power and, in some cities, also water supply, forcing Ukrainian authorities to introduce rolling power outages across the country. Ukrainian armed forces also attacked electricity infrastructure in Russian-occupied territory of Ukraine. Meanwhile according to public statements by Russian authorities on individual incidents in October, at least 90 people were killed and 306 injured in occupied territory and at least 59 people were killed and 402 injured in the Russian Federation as a result of Ukrainian attacks, but HRMMU is not able to verify the figures because of lack of access and independent sources of information. In territory of Ukraine occupied by Russia HRMMU verified that at least 12 civilians were killed and 18 injured. In all 17,994 civilians have been killed and 57,854 have been injured in Ukraine since Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February of 2022. How stories are ranked and compared

Articles about the same event are grouped into one story by text similarity, across topics. Each story is scored 0–100 from five parts: independent-source corroboration (30%), source quality (20%), recency (15%), escalation and casualty language (25%), and the topic’s severity (10%). Syndicated copies and republisher sites count as one source, not many. Only stories whose best source is an established outlet or better can appear in Top developments. Stories are never hidden for their source — they are ranked lower.

Source categories are functional, not political:

  • wire primary · weight 1
  • established · weight 0.7
  • press release · weight 0.4
  • state affiliated · weight 0.3
  • advocacy opinion · weight 0.3
  • aggregator · weight 0.15
  • unclassified · weight 0.4

This is an automated heuristic. Grouping is approximate (occasionally two events are merged or one is split), scores measure how much and how credibly something is being reported, not how true or important it ultimately is, and trend arrows reflect news coverage volume, not events on the ground.
