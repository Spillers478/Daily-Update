AMMAN — Jordan’s military said it had struck “terrorist organizations” in neighboring Syria on Monday in retaliation for a deadly jihadist attack on a church last week, hours after authorities arrested three men they accused of the killings. Syrian authorities reported civilian casualties from the raids, with health officials in southern Daraa province saying six people, including three children and a woman, were killed. Syria’s civil defense agency reported seven deaths in total, six people in Daraa and one in neighboring Sweida province. On Friday, gunmen killed nine people during a funeral service at the Greek Orthodox Saint George’s Church in the village of Hammoud in Karak governorate, southern Jordan. Karak is home to a significant Christian community in Muslim-majority Jordan, where Christians make up between 2 and 3 percent of the population. Maj. Gen. Abdullah al-Maaytah, Jordan’s director of public security, on Monday named the three detained suspects as Hazza Zaidan Mohammed, Meshal Zaidan Mohammed and Ahmad Sami Hussein. The trio — two of them brothers and the other related by marriage — were cornered at an abandoned house where they had barricaded themselves inside and opened fire on security forces. “All three terrorists have been injured. They have been taken to [the] hospital and are still receiving treatment,” Maaytah told a press conference. ‘We will show them’ One of the suspects was in critical condition, Jordan’s state broadcaster Al-Mamlaka reported. There were no casualties among the security forces, it added. “This country must remain an oasis of security and stability, and we affirm that Jordan will remain impervious to anyone who dares to challenge it,” Maaytah said. Under Jordanian law, murder is punishable by death by hanging. A number of weapons and a large quantity of ammunition were seized from the three suspects, the Public Security Directorate said. Jordan’s King Abdullah II said in a post on X that security forces “chased them to the very end.” “We vowed the terrorists would not escape punishment, and no proponent of dark ideologies that divide Jordanians will be tolerated,” he wrote. Earlier, the king visited wounded victims of the church attack in the hospital, dressed in military fatigues and carrying a pistol on his hip. “We will show them,” he vowed. Interior Minister Mazen al-Faraya told Al-Mamlaka on Monday that the attackers were all Jordanians and “had been holed up in a building in one of the villages near the site of the attack.” “There is no indication of any external contacts or collaboration with foreign parties,” he said. Government communications chief Mohammad al-Momani said “we stand ready and will strike with an iron fist, without mercy or leniency, against anyone who attempts to undermine Jordan’s security.” Attacks on houses of worship in Jordan are rare. In 2016, an attack in Karak targeted a security center and police patrols, leaving 10 people dead. On Friday, Prime Minister Jafar Hassan ordered a three-day period of national mourning for the victims of the church attack, with flags to be flown at half-staff over government and public institutions. Eight of the nine victims were laid to rest on Sunday next to the church in a funeral that drew a huge crowd amid a large security presence, AFP images showed. The ninth person, Mohammed al-Qasous, was buried in south Amman on Monday.

He said one of the key lessons from Melissa is the need for greater coordination among the police, the parish council and other relief organisations to prevent duplication in the distribution of supplies while ensuring that no affected household is overlooked. “We wanted to stop the duplication of goods and one family getting and another didn’t get,” said Gager, while crediting Parish Disaster Coordinator Dion Hylton Lewis with helping to improve the arrangement to ensure that relief supplies were distributed fairly. Ebola: 20 contacts released from quarantine in Kenya, amid vaccine ‘hope’ in Democratic Republic of the (DR) Congo
Africa Newsroom ·

They were discharged on Monday, according to the UN World Health Organization (WHO), which has been assisting the Kenyan authorities with Ebola preparedness since the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) declared an outbreak on 15 May. Another 98 contacts remain in quarantine. No risk to the community The people who are now back at home remained asymptomatic during quarantine and underwent standard protocols, including a final health assessment, counselling on post-discharge precautions and follow-up where required before being released. “We ask communities to welcome them home warmly,” said Dr Patrick Amoth, Kenya’s director general for health. “Having completed 21 days without symptoms, they pose no risk of infection to anyone.” The WHO representative to Kenya, Dr Neema Rusibamayila Kimambo, echoed his message. “People who complete quarantine have helped protect their families, their communities and the whole country,” she said. Rush to trace contacts Kenya confirmed its first Ebola case on 6 October – a Kenyan man who had been working in the DRC, where the caseload is approaching the 10,000 mark. He flew back to Kenya via Uganda on 3 October and died in a Nairobi hospital two days later. The Ministry of Health conducted robust contact tracing of anyone who may have been exposed, including family members, airline passengers and crew, airport workers and medical personnel. By 22 October, all contacts were traced – both in Kenya and abroad – and placed in quarantine. As of Monday, eight contacts had developed symptoms and were confirmed positive for Ebola disease through laboratory testing. They were immediately transferred to the Ebola treatment centre in Nairobi. Praise and support In total, Kenya has reported nine confirmed Ebola cases and two deaths. All are part of one connected group and there is no evidence that the virus has spread to the community. “WHO commends the Ministry of Health, the Kenya National Public Health Institute, national and county health teams for their work in responding quickly to every new case,” said Dr. Kimambo. The UN agency continues to support Kenya in all aspects of Ebola response, including coordination, disease surveillance, contact tracing, laboratory testing, case management, infection prevention and control, health screening at points of entry, risk communication and community engagement. Although there is no licensed vaccine or specific treatment for Bundibugyo virus disease, WHO stressed that early detection and supportive care improve chances of recovery. DRC vaccine trial Meanwhile, “a crucial clinical trial” was launched in the DRC on Friday to determine if vaccines can provide protection against the disease, said WHO’s Dr. Meg Doherty, speaking from the eastern city of Bunia to journalists in Geneva. The TUMAINI trial platform is designed so that several vaccine candidates can be evaluated within one research framework using a strategy called the ring vaccination approach involving contacts of people with a confirmed case of Ebola. “Tumaini actually means ‘hope’ in Swahili, and is giving us some hope,” said Dr. Doherty, director of WHO’s Department of Science for Health. A moving experience The first vaccine under evaluation is Ervebo which has been effective against Ebola caused by the Zaire virus. It is not yet known if it will have any protective effect against the Bundibugyo strain. Dr. Doherty attended the launch, where four people were vaccinated. “It was moving to see this trial begin, just as it has been deeply sad to see the effect of this outbreak on communities,” she said. “I lived and worked in DRC nearly 40 years ago, and it is quite heartbreaking to see this beautiful country challenged by outbreaks on top of humanitarian crises.” Evaluations and recommendations Last week, WHO convened technical advisory groups that evaluated the evidence on two vaccines designed specifically against Bundibugyo virus: ChAdOx1, developed by the University of Oxford with the Serum Institute of India, and mRNA-1469 developed by the American company Moderna. Recommendations will be published this week. “In the meantime, rings are being set up, with people who have a family member sick with Ebola, or who have been exposed to the virus another way,” she said, noting that two rings are currently in place and several more are being prepared. “Community members are already walking into the site asking to be enrolled, highlighting the intense need for an efficacious vaccine,” she added. ‘An important step’ Dr. Doherty explained that a decade ago, WHO and partners used the ring vaccination approach in Guinea during the West African Ebola outbreak to help establish the efficacy of the Ervebo vaccine. “Today, scientists are building on that experience to seek answers about the Bundibugyo virus,” she said. The trial is led by the Congolese authorities and is integrated into the ongoing response to the outbreak. It is being managed by the National Public Health and Biomedical Research Institute, known by the French acronym IRNRB. “We do not yet know for certain when results will begin to emerge. We expect it will take several months,” she said. “But already, having this trial in place is an important step towards gathering scientific data that will help policy makers reach decisions on vaccine use, and we anticipate, to protect people in this outbreak and future outbreaks.” Dr Doherty noted that several other vaccines are under development, “and as they go through their initial studies, if they look promising, they too could be brought into this vaccine platform trial.” Distributed by APO Group on behalf of UN News.

How stories are ranked and compared

Articles about the same event are grouped into one story by text similarity, across topics. Each story is scored 0–100 from five parts: independent-source corroboration (30%), source quality (20%), recency (15%), escalation and casualty language (25%), and the topic’s severity (10%). Syndicated copies and republisher sites count as one source, not many. Only stories whose best source is an established outlet or better can appear in Top developments. Stories are never hidden for their source — they are ranked lower.

Source categories are functional, not political:

  • wire primary · weight 1
  • established · weight 0.7
  • press release · weight 0.4
  • state affiliated · weight 0.3
  • advocacy opinion · weight 0.3
  • aggregator · weight 0.15
  • unclassified · weight 0.4

This is an automated heuristic. Grouping is approximate (occasionally two events are merged or one is split), scores measure how much and how credibly something is being reported, not how true or important it ultimately is, and trend arrows reflect news coverage volume, not events on the ground.
