Argentina: NEA Flooding - DREF Operation (MDRAR026)
Reliefweb reassigned from Migration / Refugee Crisis·

Please refer to the attached file. Description of the Event Date of event 03-11-2026 What happened, where and when? Since 9 October 26, extraordinary rainfall has been recorded in the upper basin of the Paraná River, associated with the El Niño phenomenon. In the west of the state of Paraná (Brazil), more than 250 mm fell in 96 hours, and towards the end of October, new rains of up to 350 mm were recorded on the Iguazú basin and the Itaipu reservoir. This caused simultaneous flooding of the Iguazú, Paraná and Uruguay rivers. On October 14, the Iguazú River reached a flow of 16,200 m³/s, about eight times its average, and discharges from the Yacyretá dam reached about 35,000 m³/s. In Ayolas (Paraguay), upstream of the Argentine section, the Paraná River reached 6.43 m in October, the highest height for that month in 55 years. As of 19 October 2026, the Paraná River exceeded evacuation levels in Corrientes Capital, Itatí, Paso de la Patria, Barranqueras and Isla del Cerrito, and the flood wave advanced downstream towards Goya, Reconquista and La Paz. The number of displaced people went from 240 (16 October) to 2,296 (21 October) and to 4,190 as of 3 November 2026, according to the Argentine Red Cross's Situation Report No. 6: 2,239 in Chaco, 1,802 in Corrientes and 149 in Santa Fe, in 23 localities. Data consolidated by government authorities record 3,903 displaced people in 21 localities, of which 2,005 (51 per cent) are self-evacuated. In total, the authorities estimate that more than 5,028 people have been affected, 2 people have died (Bella Vista, Corrientes) and 1 person has been injured. In the Chaco, the impact is concentrated in the Gran Resistencia. Puerto Vilelas accounts for 60 per cent of the province's victims (1,350 people), followed by Colonia Benítez (450), Barranqueras (253) and Isla del Cerrito (142). Municipal records and the field survey of the Argentine Red Cross show a greater impact in Barranqueras. Isla del Cerrito continues to be isolated by land due to the closure of Provincial Route No. 63: essential transfers, health care and evacuations are made by boat. The internal and coastal roads of Colonia Benítez, Tres Bocas, Isla Soto and other places are disabled by flooding and mud. The catchment of the water treatment plants continues to be affected by camalots, turbidity and sediments, and the provincial water company maintains a restricted distribution or with reduced pressure at the peripheral points. In Corrientes, there are 1,802 displaced people in 12 coastal municipalities, of which 1,393 (77 per cent) are self-evacuated. The most affected localities are Corrientes Capital (672 people, in riverside neighborhoods such as La Tosquera), Itatí (518) and Paso de la Patria (228), where the rupture of an embankment affected more than 300 homes and pumping and containment work continues. In Santa Fe, the flood has already affected Las Toscas, Tacuarendí, Reconquista and Santa Fe Capital, with 149 people displaced. Although in recent days the river has registered decreases of a few centimeters in the upper section, the humanitarian impact does not improve: it is a pause within a scenario of prolonged high waters. As of 2 November 2026, the Paraná River marked 7.11 m in Corrientes (evacuation level: 7.00 m), after a peak of 7.35 m, and 7.00 m in Barranqueras (evacuation: 6.50 m). On 3 November, it dropped just 5 cm in Barranqueras. At that rate, the river will remain above evacuation levels for weeks and families will not be able to return to their homes. Meanwhile, the flood wave is advancing downstream: Goya (6.09 m) and Reconquista (5.91 m) exceed their evacuation levels, La Paz (5.86 m) exceeded the alert level, and the National Water Institute (INA) forecasts that Paraná and Santa Fe will reach or exceed the alert level in the second week of November. Added to this is the Paraguay River, which remains at 7.55 m, and the Bermejo River, which rose 18 cm on 3 November. The most worrying factor is the rain on an already saturated basin. For 3 to 4 November 2026, the National Meteorological Service (SMN) issued an orange alert for strong storms, some severe, throughout the province of Corrientes, with accumulated 40 to 80 mm and gusts of more than 90 km/h, and yellow alert in Chaco (20 to 40 mm), Misiones and Formosa. The Yacyretá Binational Entity forecasts between 2 and 4 November 2026 accumulated 20 to 50 mm in much of the Paraná basin, with up to 90 mm in some sectors. In southern Brazil, forecasts for November anticipate above-normal rainfall, with the highest excesses in the state of Paraná and risk of flooding over the unregulated basin of Alto Paraná and Iguazú. In Paraguay, rains and storms persist on the left bank of the Paraná and the Paraguay River basin, and the Paraná remains at 5.35 m in Ayolas, above alert and flood levels, with more than 3,200 people evacuated in that city alone. These figures are from a separate record and are not included in the displaced persons totals submitted for Argentina. As the soils have excess accumulated moisture, each new rain immediately drains into the rivers. That is why the National Water Institute (INA) warns that it is likely to observe new spikes and a second wave of flood in the Paraná River, due to the volume in transit from the Upper Paraná and the rains on the Paraguay River basin and the Itaipu contribution area, with a new flood transit from the Iguazú River. It also forecasts that the Uruguay River will approach the alert level again in São Tomé and San Javier between 6 and 8 November 2026. The Yacyretá Binational Entity also anticipates a new increase in flow after the current slight decrease. On a seasonal scale, the SMN forecasts above-normal rainfall on the Littoral for the November–January quarter. NOAA's Climate Prediction Center anticipates a strong to very strong El Niño, with a high probability of reaching historic intensity and persisting at least until the January–March 2027 quarter. In this context, an increase in hydrometeorological events (intense rains, severe storms, and floods) is expected until February–March 2027 throughout the La Plata Basin, including the Uruguay River and the Paraná Delta. The Civil Defense Directorate of Corrientes describes a long-term emergency, until March or April 2027, and the Government of Chaco estimates stays of 3 to 4 months in evacuation centers. With this scenario, some 200 additional families are projected to be affected (around 1,000 people) in the prioritized areas in the coming weeks, in addition to possible new effects in Santa Fe, Entre Ríos, and Buenos Aires. Exceptional nature of the event. The magnitude of this flood is exceptional for the basin and, according to official forecasts, the situation has not yet reached its peak. Upstream, the Paraná River in Ciudad del Este (Paraguay) marked the historical maximum of its series of records (32.96 m, on 15 October 2026), and the stations of Cerrito, Panchito López and Itá Corá surpassed their records of November 2023. In Ayolas, the October height was the highest in 55 years. The Iguazú River reached about 17,700 m³/s on 26 October, almost 12 times its average flow, and the Devil's Throat was closed for 20 days. The flows entering Yacyretá, of about 35,000 m³/s, are equivalent to 2.4 times the historical average of October (14,400 m³/s, series 1971–2025). In Corrientes and Barranqueras the river has remained above the evacuation level since 19 October. The most worrying thing is not the current reading but what is coming: the INA and the Yacyretá Binational Entity predict new rebounds, a second wave of floods and new increases in flow due to the rains expected over a saturated basin in northern Argentina, southern Brazil and Paraguay. Added to this is an El Niño with a high probability of reaching historic intensity, which would sustain high waters until February-March 2027, in the middle of the dengue season. To date there is no declaration of national or provincial emergency. The National System for Integrated Risk Management (SINAGIR) maintains the Red Alert for the region. The Chaco alert system maintains the red alert in Barranqueras, Puerto Vilelas and Isla del Cerrito, and in Corrientes there are 11 coastal municipalities on alert. Reconquista (Santa Fe) has a municipal emergency, and on 31 October 2026 a bill to declare a water emergency in the Chaco entered the Senate of the Nation, which has not yet been discussed.

Senate Republicans shift $69B in defense reconciliation funding to regular appropriations
Insidedefense ·

Senate Republicans are proposing to move nearly $69 billion in defense funding sought by the Trump administration through budget reconciliation into the regular appropriations process as part of a nearly $1.1 trillion fiscal year 2027 defense spending bill that makes major investments in munitions production, missile defense, drones, shipbuilding and military modernization. The Senate Appropriations Committee's Republican majority is providing the money as discretionary rather than the one-time mandatory spending originally requested by the administration. The committee argues that the approach would provide more stable, predictable funding for critical military capabilities, according to an explanatory statement released with the bill. The proposal reflects growing skepticism among Senate appropriators about the White House's plan to secure $350 billion in additional defense funding through reconciliation. Appropriations Chairwoman Susan Collins (R-ME) and other Republicans have questioned the cost and scope of another party-line package, while some defense hawks, including Senate Armed Services Committee Chairman Roger Wicker (R-MS), continue to press for the full request. Though Senate Republicans have not formally abandoned the reconciliation effort, the appropriations proposal would shift a substantial portion of the administration's priorities into the regular spending process. Sen. Patty Murray (D-WA), the committee's top Democrat and likely chairwoman next year when her party takes the majority, said she objects to the proposal. “This bill delivers a truly massive windfall for Pete Hegseth’s Pentagon -- meeting President Trump’s eye-popping request -- while doing next to nothing to rein in this president who is waging illegal wars,” she said. “We cannot and should not accept the roadmap Senate Republicans have laid out which would hurl hundreds of billions of dollars more at the Pentagon while doing exceptionally little to help families get by.” Munitions and missile defense The committee's proposal includes $47.2 billion for additional munitions procurement and acceleration efforts, $35.6 billion for air and missile defense capabilities and $6.2 billion for drone and counter-drone programs. The bill provides $40.6 billion for new multiyear procurement contracts covering nine munitions: Advanced Medium-Range Air-to-Air Missiles, Guided Multiple Launch Rocket Systems, Patriot Advanced Capability-3 Missile Segment Enhancement interceptors, Precision Strike Missiles Increment 1, Standard Missile-3 Block IB and Block IIA, Standard Missile-6, Terminal High Altitude Area Defense missiles and Tomahawk cruise missiles. The proposal also includes $1.5 billion for additional quantities of Long Range Anti-Ship Missiles and Joint Air-to-Surface Standoff Missile-Extended Range missiles under existing multiyear procurement agreements. Another $1 billion would procure additional Joint Advanced Tactical Missiles, while $1.9 billion would support systems made by new industry entrants, including Blackbeard Ground Launch, the Multi-mission Affordable Capacity Effector, the Family of Affordable Mass Missile, low-cost cruise missiles and containerized munitions. An additional $1.4 billion would replenish inventories of weapons including Joint Air-to-Ground Missiles, Evolved Sea Sparrow Missiles, Advanced Precision Kill Weapon System rockets, 30mm proximity ammunition, Joint Direct Attack Munitions and general-purpose bombs. For air and missile defense, the committee provides $35.6 billion, including $32 billion for additional defensive interceptors, $812 million for two additional THAAD radars and $170 million for air and missile defense software development and integration. The proposal separately includes $9.4 billion in discretionary funding for Golden Dome initiatives, which the administration had requested as mandatory spending. The investment is intended to advance protection of critical infrastructure in the United States and its territories against ballistic, hypersonic and cruise missiles, as well as other advanced aerial threats. The White House, however, has requested $17.5 billion for Golden Dome in FY-27. F-35 and drone investments The committee provides $11.1 billion for Joint Strike Fighter procurement, modifications and sustainment, along with $2.9 billion for research, development, test and evaluation. The funding includes $5.7 billion originally requested as mandatory spending. The bill adds $2.4 billion for 15 additional F-35Cs, $1 billion for five additional F-35Bs and $704 million for five additional F-35As. For drones and counter-drone capabilities, the committee provides $6.2 billion for research, development and procurement. That includes $3 billion in additional discretionary funding for the Defense Autonomous Working Group. The administration, meanwhile, was seeking almost $55 billion for DAWG in FY-27 in the hopes of establishing a new combatant command. Shipbuilding and industrial base The bill provides $42 billion for shipbuilding, including $1.9 billion for a second DDG-51 destroyer. The committee provides $329 million for the Submarine Industrial Base program and $595 million for the Surface Ship Industrial Base program to improve supply chain resilience. The proposal includes incremental funding for one Columbia-class submarine, two Virginia-class submarines, one aircraft carrier refueling and complex overhaul, one FF(X) frigate, one LPD Flight II amphibious transport dock and one LHA replacement ship. Service-specific investments and space The Army would receive $61.3 billion for procurement and $19.4 billion for research and development, including $360 million for CH-47s, $185 million for the Improved Turbine Engine Program and $2.1 billion for Future Long-Range Assault Aircraft modernization. The Navy would receive $113.4 billion for procurement and $29.9 billion for research and development, including $250 million for the Sea-Launched Cruise Missile-Nuclear, $350 million for sonobuoys and additional funding for ship missile defenses, Coyote systems and patrol boats. The Air Force would receive $78.9 billion for procurement and $67.4 billion for research and development. Major additions include $1.4 billion for eight C-130Js for the Air National Guard, funding for F-15EX enhancements and $150 million for Next Generation Adaptive Propulsion. The proposal also supports the administration's $4.5 billion Sentinel request. The Space Force would receive $13.8 billion for procurement and $32.3 billion for research and development, including $3.3 billion for the Space Data Network, $2 billion for space-based moving target indicator capability and $7.1 billion for 54 National Security Space Launch missions. Security cooperation would receive $5.4 billion, including $1 billion for partnerships with Taiwan and the Philippines, $750 million for Ukraine and $175 million for the Baltic states. The bill provides another $500 million for U.S.-Israeli missile defense cooperation, plus funding for counter-drone and emerging technology cooperation and subterranean operations.

Syndicated copies and republisher sites count as one source, not many. Only stories whose best source is an established outlet or better can appear in Top developments.
