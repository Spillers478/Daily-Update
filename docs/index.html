Ukraine: Protection of Civilians in Armed Conflict - October 2026

In Ukraine Civilians Die at Alarming Rates Amid Intensifying Attacks, UN Human Rights Officials Say Kyiv, November 17, 2026 -- Powerful weapons of war kill ever growing numbers of civilians across Ukraine, the UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine (HRMMU) said today. Attacks in October alone left at least 372 civilians dead and 1,981 injured HRMMU reported in its monthly update. The report notes that the number of killed and injured in October of this year was 95 per cent higher than in October 2025. The latest figures show that this year has seen significantly higher casualties than previous years. The alarming trend continued in November which saw one of the deadliest single attacks for civilians since the start of Russia's full-scale invasion. On 2 November multiple bombs struck the premises of a school, including an underground shelter in Komyshuvakha in south-eastern Ukraine, where civilians had gathered to receive humanitarian assistance. Ukrainian authorities announced on 16 November that 72 people had died in the attack and that four others were still missing. HRMMU continues to verify the identity and status of those killed, but has already confirmed that dozens of them were civilians, including local residents, local administration staff and emergency workers. "The first ten months of this year have seen more civilian deaths than all of 2025, which in turn was worse than the year before," said Danielle Bell who heads HRMMU. "The world should not become numb to the deadly toll the war exacts on Ukraine's civilians," she said. Long-range weapons (missiles and drones) remain the leading cause of civilian casualties, accounting for more than half of those killed and injured. In October, casualties from aerial glide bombs increased, with the number of civilians killed doubling compared with September, reaching 92 killed and 331 injured. Aerial glide bombs and short-range drones accounted for nearly equal shares of civilian casualties, 18 and 19 per cent, respectively. In October, Russian armed forces significantly intensified attacks on energy infrastructure across Ukraine as the country braces for its fifth winter at war. The attacks damaged electricity and heating infrastructure, temporarily disrupting power and, in some cities, also water supply, forcing Ukrainian authorities to introduce rolling power outages across the country. Ukrainian armed forces also attacked electricity infrastructure in Russian-occupied territory of Ukraine. Meanwhile according to public statements by Russian authorities on individual incidents in October, at least 90 people were killed and 306 injured in occupied territory and at least 59 people were killed and 402 injured in the Russian Federation as a result of Ukrainian attacks, but HRMMU is not able to verify the figures because of lack of access and independent sources of information. In territory of Ukraine occupied by Russia HRMMU verified that at least 12 civilians were killed and 18 injured. In all 17,994 civilians have been killed and 57,854 have been injured in Ukraine since Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February of 2022. Deadly bus crash sparks anti-government protests in Iran's Kurdish town
Saudi Gazette reassigned from Mass Uprising / Regime-Threatening Unrest·

TEHRAN — Hundreds of people took to the streets of Abdanan in predominantly Kurdish Ilam province in southwestern Iran on Tuesday to protest the deaths of 13 oil and gas workers in a bus crash. The Fars news agency, which is close to the Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps (IRGC), said crowds grew to about 1,000 people in a rare display of anti-government anger that comes against the backdrop of warnings by rights groups of a wartime crackdown on dissent in the country. The bus was carrying employees of the NGL Dehloran oil and gas company when it overturned on the evening of November 14. Thirteen people were killed and 15 were injured. The protesters demanded the resignation of company officials blamed for the accident. Authorities have blamed several parties for the crash. Ilam Province’s traffic police chief cited negligence by the bus driver, the company violating its bus rental contract, and road maintenance failures. Prosecutors have filed charges against the NGL Dehloran CEO and the provincial road maintenance director. Fars reported that after a while the protesters’ chants took on a “political tone.” Online footage showed protesters calling on officials they blamed for the crash to resign. Later videos appeared to show crowds chanting “Death to the dictator,” a slogan aimed at the supreme leader that has become a rallying cry of Iran’s protest movements in the past. Abdanan’s prosecutor, Amrollah Meysaei, warned protesters that the authorities would respond firmly.“Those who seek to exploit this incident to disrupt public order and inflame people’s emotions will be dealt with decisively,” he told the judiciary’s Mizan news agency. It also claimed that some protesters tried to “damage public property.”In January, Iran witnessed some of the largest anti-government protests since the 1979 Islamic Revolution. Security forces shot thousands of protesters in the streets and arrested thousands more. Abdanan — a largely Kurdish town with a population of some 24,000 people — was a hotspot of unrest in January. The Hengaw Organization for Human Rights said at least seven people were detained in Abdanan after Tuesday’s protests — though two were later released. A lawmaker from the province denied there had been any arrests, the Tasnim news agency said on Wednesday. No tsunami threat was reported, and there were no immediate reports of significant damage. The strongest shaking, measuring lower 5, was recorded in Minamata City, Ashikita Town, Tsunagi Town, Kuma Village and Asagiri Town in Kumamoto Prefecture. Seismic intensity 4 was observed across a broad area of central and southern Kyushu, including Yatsushiro, Hitoyoshi, Kamiamakusa, Uki and Amakusa cities in Kumamoto Prefecture, as well as Miyazaki and Kobayashi cities in Miyazaki Prefecture. Several municipalities in Kagoshima Prefecture, including Izumi, Satsuma, Isa, Yusui and Nagashima, also experienced intensity 4 shaking. The meteorological agency warned that seismic activity could remain above normal levels for some time and urged residents to maintain their usual preparations for earthquakes and tsunamis. The earthquake occurred near the coast of Ashikita Town, in an area where seismic activity has remained elevated since the magnitude 7.1 Kumamoto earthquake on July 28th, 2026. That earlier disaster, officially designated the 2026 Kumamoto Earthquake, produced a maximum seismic intensity of 7 and ruptured an estimated 30- to 40-kilometer section of underground rock extending from south of Kumamoto City toward Yatsushiro City. The November 19th earthquake occurred farther south, near the northern end of the Yatsushiro Sea section of the Hinagu Fault Zone, raising concerns about the possibility of further seismic activity along parts of the fault system that did not rupture during the July earthquake. An examination of earthquake locations between October 17th and November 18th showed numerous tremors extending southward from the area affected by the July earthquake. The broader zone of seismic activity stretches approximately 70 to 80 kilometers, substantially longer than the section believed to have ruptured during the magnitude 7.1 event. This distribution suggests that some of the earthquakes occurring since July 28th may have been triggered by changes in underground stress associated with the earlier earthquake, rather than being conventional aftershocks confined to the original rupture area. How stories are ranked and compared

Articles about the same event are grouped into one story by text similarity, across topics. Each story is scored 0–100 from five parts: independent-source corroboration (30%), source quality (20%), recency (15%), escalation and casualty language (25%), and the topic’s severity (10%). Syndicated copies and republisher sites count as one source, not many. Only stories whose best source is an established outlet or better can appear in Top developments. Stories are never hidden for their source — they are ranked lower.

Source categories are functional, not political:

  • established · weight 0.7
  • press release · weight 0.4
  • state affiliated · weight 0.3
  • advocacy opinion · weight 0.3
  • aggregator · weight 0.15
  • unclassified · weight 0.4

This is an automated heuristic. Grouping is approximate (occasionally two events are merged or one is split), scores measure how much and how credibly something is being reported, not how true or important it ultimately is, and trend arrows reflect news coverage volume, not events on the ground.
