Hormuz 'Closed', Bab El-Mandeb Falls: Three US Carriers Off Iran By Monday On Sunday we had a few developments in the Middle East... Iran's Revolutionary Guards declared the Strait of Hormuz "closed," Saudi-backed forces in Yemen gave up the coast at the Bab el-Mandeb to the Houthis, and the New York Times reported that the USS Theodore Roosevelt will join two other American carriers in the region within the next day. Yemen's Saudi-backed forces pulled out of the area overlooking the Bab el-Mandeb Strait on Sunday, leaving the Iran-aligned Houthis in control of the coast at the Red Sea's southern gate and ending a six-week counteroffensive that was supposed to take it back. The Southern Armed Forces, the main force fighting for the internationally recognized government in the south, announced "a redeployment operation for its forces from Dhubab Airport and the Bab el-Mandeb area," Reuters reported. "This step is taken based on the priority of securing the capital, Aden," the statement said, referring to the southern port city that has served as the government's base since the Houthis took Sanaa in 2015. The units have fallen back to al-Suqyah, just south of Dhubab and about 150 km (95 miles) west of Aden, and are still trying to push the Houthis off nearby stretches of coast, according to military sources cited by Reuters. Neither the Houthis nor Saudi Arabia commented right away. The retreat erases almost everything the Saudi-backed campaign won since October. The Houthis swept down the coast in September, taking the port of Mocha, as we reported at the time, then Dhubab and the islands in the strait. A counteroffensive backed by Saudi air power retook Dhubab last month, but it never dislodged the Houthis from the area or recaptured Mocha. Government military spokesman Majed Al-Nuzaili welcomed the move and called for international help to secure the strait, citing its importance to shipping and global trade, Arab News reported. He described defending Aden and lifting the siege of Taiz as "linked national responsibilities." In the highlands south of Taiz, Houthi forces sit just outside Tur al-Bahah in Lahj governorate, about 80 km (50 miles) from Aden. Taiz, Yemen's third-largest city, has been cut off since early October; it holds about half of the 300,000 people displaced by the latest fighting, and aid cannot get in while civilians cannot get out. The southern forces said the redeployment would also back the push to break that siege and end what they called "blockade and starvation policies," The National reported. Yemen's armed forces said they carried out 906 strikes on Houthi positions on Sunday, 41 of them airstrikes, along with drone, rocket and artillery fire on fronts in Marib, al-Jawf and Taiz. With Iran disrupting traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, Saudi Arabia has leaned on the Red Sea to move its crude, piping it west on the East-West pipeline to export terminals at Yanbu. The Houthis have hit that route repeatedly: the pipeline was struck by drones again on Nov. 9, and a day later the Houthis launched another salvo at Riyadh's airport and pumping stations along the line. The Saudi-led coalition said it shot down two Houthi ballistic missiles fired toward Riyadh on Sunday, the same day the southern forces announced their pullback. Earlier this month the Houthis went further, launching cross-border ground raids into Saudi territory. Yemen's civil war had been largely frozen since a 2022 truce before it reignited this summer as a spillover of the Iran war the US and Israel launched in February. The Houthis' gains and their attacks on Saudi Arabia and Red Sea shipping have strengthened Iran's position in that wider fight, Reuters noted. The retreat comes two days after President Trump said in an interview that Saudi Arabia is "disintegrating." The Roosevelt's arrival would put three US carrier strike groups within striking range of Iran at once. The Roosevelt would link up with the USS George H.W. Bush, on station in the region since April, and the Japan-based USS George Washington, there since August, according to Stars and Stripes. Its strike group brings Carrier Air Wing 11, the cruiser USS Chosin, the destroyers USS Paul Hamilton and USS Decatur and more than 5,000 service members. Three carriers would give the US up to 144 front-line strike fighters, F/A-18s and F-35s, against the roughly 96 combat aircraft Capital Alpha's Byron Callan counts on the two decks now. The carrier report landed the same day Iran fired six drones at a US air defense radar in Bahrain, its second attack on the kingdom in three days, and the Revolutionary Guards declared that "The Strait of Hormuz is closed," as we reported earlier Sunday. Bahrain said its air defenses brought the drones down before they reached their targets. The Roosevelt left San Diego on Sept. 27, with Navy officials telling families to plan on eight months away, and as we noted at the time, it was expected to relieve the George Washington rather than add to the force. Srinagar, Nov 15: Dense construction, narrow roads and closely packed buildings make downtown Srinagar the city's most vulnerable area to earthquake damage, with 23 of its 69 municipal wards falling in the high and very high vulnerability categories, a scientific study has found. Nearly 17 sq km, or about 7 percent of the city, falls in these categories. Nine wards, covering 1.28 percent of the city, were classified as very highly vulnerable and 14 wards, accounting for 5.7 percent, as highly vulnerable. Another 19 wards had moderate vulnerability, 17 low and 10 very low. High building density, narrow streets, a shortage of open spaces and the risk of buildings colliding during earthquake shaking were identified as major factors behind the vulnerability. Uptown areas were relatively less vulnerable because of wider roads and lower building density. About 65 percent of Srinagar's buildings face a high risk of structural pounding with neighbouring structures on at least one side. This occurs when closely spaced buildings strike each other during an earthquake. Nearly 98 percent of the city's buildings are masonry structures, including 86.4 percent residential and 7.1 percent commercial buildings. Educational, religious, health, defence and other structures account for the remaining 6.5 percent. Aali Kadal has the highest proportion of masonry buildings at 99.8 percent. Two-storey buildings account for 50 percent of the total and those with three or more stories 42 percent. Only 8 percent are single-storey, while 307 buildings, or 0.12 percent, have more than three floors. Downtown also has some of the city's highest building densities. Against a citywide average of 10 buildings per hectare, 17 downtown wards have more than 15. Jamia Masjid leads with 46 buildings per hectare, followed by Tarabal at 39 and Aali Kadal at 30. Bud Dal has the lowest density at one building per hectare. Narrow roads could further impede evacuation and rescue operations. Six wards — Syed Ali Akbar, Bana Mohalla, Bud Dal, Zindshah Sahib, Ganpatyar, and Safa Kadal have average road widths of less than 7 feet. The researchers linked the city's vulnerability partly to unplanned urban growth and gaps in implementing successive master plans. Srinagar's population rose from about 2.50 lakh in 1961 to 15 lakh in 2011, increasing pressure on its urban infrastructure. They recommended restricting construction in densely populated, highly vulnerable areas, protecting open spaces for emergency evacuation and retrofitting vulnerable buildings. "Since green and open spaces are used as evacuation places, it is strongly advised that new construction in these areas, as well as the development of these spaces, must be avoided," the study states. The researchers also called for emergency action plans, fire services, shelters and specialised medical facilities in vulnerable areas, along with stricter enforcement of building codes, earthquake-resistant construction and public awareness programs. The assessment covers the physical vulnerability of buildings and infrastructure but does not examine residents' socioeconomic vulnerability. The researchers recommended including these factors in future assessments. The study was conducted by Midhat Fayaz, Prof Shakil Ahmad Romshoo, Irfan Rashid, and Rakesh Chandra. Using Cartosat-2 satellite imagery, geographic information system (GIS) mapping and field surveys, they assessed six parameters across Srinagar's 69 wards and collected 8000 ground-validation points. ASEAN leaders maintain Myanmar’s non-political representation until progress on peace plan
Manila Bulletin reassigned from Migration / Refugee Crisis·

Amid continuing concerns over Myanmar’s political crisis and ongoing armed conflicts, leaders of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) agreed to maintain the country’s non-political representation at ASEAN Summits and Foreign Ministers’ Meetings until there is meaningful progress in implementing the bloc’s Five-Point Consensus (5PC). In a statement adopted at the ASEAN Leaders’ Review and Decision on the Implementation of the Five-Point Consensus in Manila on Nov. 15, the regional leaders reaffirmed their commitment to the peace plan as the main reference for addressing the situation in Myanmar. They also reaffirmed previous ASEAN decisions on Myanmar’s non-political representation at the bloc’s high-level meetings. The leaders pushed to continue upholding the collective ASEAN decisions of the 40th, 41st, 43rd, 44th, 45th, 46th, 47th, and 48th ASEAN Summits regarding Myanmar’s non-political representation at ASEAN Summits and ASEAN Foreign Ministers’ Meetings "until there is meaningful progress" in the implementation of the 5PC. Myanmar’s participation in other meetings of ASEAN sectoral ministerial bodies or mechanisms, however, “should be unrestricted at all levels,” the leaders said. ‘Insufficient progress’ in Myanmar peace plan The leaders expressed deep concern over the ongoing conflicts and humanitarian situation in Myanmar, as well as the insufficient progress in implementing the Five-Point Consensus. They reiterated their concern over the “insufficient progress in the implementation of the 5PC,” while acknowledging limited progress in humanitarian assistance through the ASEAN Coordinating Centre for Humanitarian Assistance on disaster management (AHA Centre). The Five-Point Consensus, adopted in April 2021, remains ASEAN’s framework for addressing the crisis in Myanmar. The leaders reiterated that the 5PC remains the main reference for addressing the political crisis and called for its full implementation to help the people of Myanmar achieve an inclusive and lasting peace. They also called on all parties involved, particularly the armed forces, security forces, and armed groups, to de-escalate violence and stop targeted attacks against civilians and civilian infrastructure. “We reiterate our call for the cessation of violence and underscored the importance of a sustained nationwide ceasefire in Myanmar,” the statement said. The leaders stressed that a ceasefire would help create a safe environment for humanitarian assistance and inclusive national dialogue. Calls for expanded humanitarian assistance ASEAN leaders also called for the safe, effective, and transparent delivery of humanitarian assistance to people affected by the ongoing armed conflicts in Myanmar. They urged the AHA Centre to ensure that assistance based on the Joint Needs Assessment directly reaches internally displaced persons (IDPs), while responding to changing humanitarian needs on the ground. The leaders likewise encouraged regional, external, and international partners to increase financial support for sustained humanitarian operations. They called on Myanmar to facilitate humanitarian access and allow the AHA Centre and other humanitarian organizations to expand assistance to underserved communities. The statement also welcomed the AHA Centre ASEAN Monitoring Team Mission to Myanmar on Oct. 28, led by Philippine Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) Secretary Ma. Theresa P. Lazaro in her capacity as Special Envoy of the ASEAN Chair on Myanmar. The mission marked the beginning of a month-long distribution of humanitarian assistance to more than 11,000 households, including communities in four new priority townships that had not previously been reached by the AHA Centre. ASEAN also welcomed Myanmar’s openness to further expanding humanitarian access to other areas. PH’s special envoy recognized for Myanmar engagements The regional leaders commended the Philippines’ efforts, led by Lazaro, to engage various stakeholders in Myanmar and advance the implementation of the 5PC. They welcomed her visits to Nay Pyi Taw on Jan. 6 and Oct. 29, 2026, as part of her mandate to meet with all parties concerned in Myanmar. They also recognized her efforts to promote inclusive dialogue and engage relevant stakeholders, including regional and international organizations. These engagements included the Myanmar Stakeholders’ Meeting in Tagaytay City, Philippines, from Jan. 19 to 20, and informal meetings with Myanmar ethnic armed organizations and representatives of the National Solidarity and Peacemaking Negotiation Committee in Pattaya, Thailand, on July 13. ASEAN leaders reaffirmed their commitment to helping Myanmar find a peaceful and durable solution to the crisis, emphasizing that the country remains an integral part of the regional bloc. Adoption of ‘calibrated approach’ to Myanmar While maintaining restrictions on Myanmar’s political representation at key ASEAN meetings, the leaders agreed to pursue a calibrated and phased approach based on developments on the ground. “Subject to sustained and concrete progress in the implementation of the 5PC by Myanmar, ASEAN may consider the gradual restoration of Myanmar’s full political representation,” the statement said. They also tasked ASEAN foreign ministers to continue constructive and principled engagement with Myanmar, including through informal discussions consistent with the Five-Point Consensus. The leaders further called for stronger coordination among the previous, current, and incoming ASEAN chairs to ensure policy continuity and sustained implementation of the peace plan. They also acknowledged ongoing discussions to establish a longer-term ASEAN Special Envoy on Myanmar to ensure continuity in the role and to develop ways to assess progress in the country. ASEAN leaders said they would review these decisions at future meetings and tasked foreign ministers to continue monitoring developments and report to the next ASEAN Summit.

For 'not at risk' countries, a sub-section (2 per cent of the total flight passengers) shall undergo post-arrival testing at random at the airport. Laboratories shall prioritise testing of samples from such travellers. How stories are ranked and compared

Articles about the same event are grouped into one story by text similarity, across topics. Each story is scored 0–100 from five parts: independent-source corroboration (30%), source quality (20%), recency (15%), escalation and casualty language (25%), and the topic’s severity (10%). Syndicated copies and republisher sites count as one source, not many. Only stories whose best source is an established outlet or better can appear in Top developments. Stories are never hidden for their source — they are ranked lower.

Source categories are functional, not political:

  • wire primary · weight 1
  • established · weight 0.7
  • press release · weight 0.4
  • state affiliated · weight 0.3
  • advocacy opinion · weight 0.3
  • aggregator · weight 0.15
  • unclassified · weight 0.4

This is an automated heuristic. Grouping is approximate (occasionally two events are merged or one is split), scores measure how much and how credibly something is being reported, not how true or important it ultimately is, and trend arrows reflect news coverage volume, not events on the ground.
